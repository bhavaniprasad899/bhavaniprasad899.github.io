📖 GRC — Governance, Risk & Compliance

GRC is the backbone of enterprise security. This section covers security frameworks, risk assessment methodologies, compliance standards, and audit preparation.

1. Governance

Governance defines how security decisions are made, who is accountable, and how policies are enforced.

Key Governance Elements

  • Security Policy: High-level document defining security goals and rules
  • Procedures: Step-by-step instructions for implementing policies
  • Standards: Mandatory requirements (e.g., password complexity)
  • Guidelines: Recommended practices (not mandatory)
  • Roles & Responsibilities: Who does what (CISO, DPO, SOC Manager)

2. Risk Management

Risk = Likelihood x Impact. Organizations must identify, assess, and mitigate risks.

Risk Assessment Process

Step 1: IDENTIFY ASSETS
- Servers, databases, applications, employee data, IP

Step 2: IDENTIFY THREATS
- Malware, insider threats, natural disasters, third-party risks

Step 3: ASSESS VULNERABILITIES
- Unpatched software, weak passwords, misconfigurations

Step 4: CALCULATE RISK
- Likelihood (1-5) x Impact (1-5) = Risk Score

Step 5: TREAT RISK
- Accept / Mitigate / Transfer / Avoid

Risk Matrix Example

                 IMPACT
              Low  Med  High
        High  Med  High  Critical
LIKELI  Med   Low  Med   High
        Low   Low  Low   Med

Risk Score Legend:
1-4   Low    → Accept
5-9   Medium → Mitigate
10-14 High   → Action required
15-25 Critical → Immediate action

3. Compliance Frameworks

ISO 27001 — Information Security Management

Clauses:
4. Context of the organization
5. Leadership
6. Planning (risk assessment)
7. Support (awareness, competence)
8. Operation (risk treatment plan)
9. Performance evaluation (audits, reviews)
10. Improvement (corrective actions)

A.15  Supplier relationships

A.17  Business continuity

NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF)

5 FUNCTIONS:
1. IDENTIFY  — Asset management, risk assessment
2. PROTECT  — Access control, data security, training
3. DETECT   — Continuous monitoring, anomaly detection
4. RESPOND  — Response planning, communication, mitigation
5. RECOVER  — Recovery planning, improvements

Tiers: Partial → Risk-Informed → Repeatable → Adaptive
Profiles: Current Profile vs Target Profile

Other Compliance Standards

  • GDPR: EU data protection — fines up to 4% of global revenue
  • PCI DSS: Payment card industry — 12 requirements for handling card data
  • HIPAA: Healthcare data protection (US)
  • SOX: Financial reporting controls (US public companies)
  • IT Act 2000: India's cyber law framework

4. Security Audits

Audit Checklist (Sample)

[ ] Password policy enforced (min 8 chars, complexity)
[ ] MFA enabled for all admin accounts
[ ] Firewall rules reviewed in last 90 days
[ ] Antivirus/malware protection active on all endpoints
[ ] Software patching within 30 days
[ ] Access reviews conducted quarterly
[ ] Encryption in transit (TLS 1.2+) and at rest
[ ] Incident response plan tested in last 12 months
[ ] Backup tested and verified
[ ] Third-party vendors assessed for security

5. Policy Templates

Acceptable Use Policy (Template)

1. PURPOSE
   This policy defines acceptable use of company IT resources.

2. SCOPE
   All employees, contractors, and third parties.

3. POLICY
   - Company devices are for business use only
   - Personal use must not interfere with work
   - Prohibited: illegal activities, harassment, piracy
   - All internet traffic may be monitored

4. VIOLATIONS
   - Violations may result in disciplinary action
   - Report violations to security@company.com

5. REVIEW
   - This policy is reviewed annually
